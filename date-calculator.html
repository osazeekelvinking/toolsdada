How the Date Calculator Works

The Date Calculator determines the precise calendar interval between two points in time or projects target calendar dates when adding or subtracting years, months, weeks, and days. It fully respects Gregorian calendar leap year variations, irregular month lengths (28, 29, 30, or 31 days), and working business day intervals.

1. Calculating Duration Between Two Dates

Calendar time is calculated using epoch timestamps and localized year-month-day calendar offsets:

  • Total Days: The absolute mathematical difference in milliseconds divided by 86,400,000 ms (24 × 60 × 60 × 1,000).
  • Business Days: Days excluding Saturdays and Sundays. In a standard full year (365 days), there are typically 260 or 261 working business days and 104 weekend days.
  • Years, Months, and Days: Borrowing arithmetic where the remainder of days borrows the exact number of days from the preceding month.

2. Gregorian Leap Year Rules

A leap year adds an extra intercalary day on February 29th to keep the calendar synchronized with Earth's orbital period around the Sun (approximately 365.2422 solar days). Under the Gregorian calendar reform of 1582:

  • Every year evenly divisible by 4 is a leap year (e.g., 2024, 2028).
  • Except end-of-century years (e.g., 1900, 2100), which are NOT leap years unless:
  • The year is also evenly divisible by 400 (e.g., 1600, 2000, 2400).
