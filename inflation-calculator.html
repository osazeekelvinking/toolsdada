How Inflation Works: The Silent Wealth Tax

Inflation is the general rise in the price of goods and services over time, resulting in a persistent decrease in the purchasing power of money. When prices increase, each individual currency unit buys fewer products. The future equivalent cost formula is:

Future Price (F) = Current Amount × (1 + r)n
Real Purchasing Power (P) = Current Amount / (1 + r)n

Where r is the annual inflation rate (expressed as a decimal) and n is the number of elapsed years.

The Rule of 72: Price Doubling Benchmark

The Rule of 72 provides a swift mathematical estimate of how long it takes for the general price level to double at a steady inflation rate. Simply divide 72 by the annual percentage rate:

  • At 2.0% Inflation (Fed target): Prices double every 36.0 years.
  • At 3.2% Inflation (US long-term average): Prices double every 22.5 years.
  • At 6.0% Inflation: Prices double every 12.0 years.
  • At 8.0% Inflation: Prices double every 9.0 years.

Nominal vs. Real Returns

Many investors mistakenly look solely at nominal returns (the raw percentage gain shown on their bank or brokerage statement). However, what truly determines financial independence is real return, defined as:

Real Return ≈ Nominal Return − Inflation Rate

If a certificate of deposit (CD) pays 4.0% interest while inflation averages 5.0%, the investor's purchasing power declines by approximately 1.0% each year, despite the positive dollar balance shown on paper.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between future cost and purchasing power?

Future cost tells you how many dollars you will need in the future to purchase a basket of goods that costs $100 today. Purchasing power tells you how much that same $100 cash bill sitting in your wallet today will actually buy in the future.

What is the Rule of 72 for inflation?

The Rule of 72 is a mental shortcut to estimate how many years it takes for prices to double due to inflation: divide 72 by the annual inflation rate. For example, at 3.0% inflation, everyday prices double in approximately 24 years (72 / 3 = 24).

What is the historical average inflation rate in the US?

Over the past 50 to 100 years, the US Consumer Price Index (CPI) has averaged approximately 3.1% to 3.3% annually. The Federal Reserve targets a long-run annual inflation benchmark of 2.0%.

How can I protect my savings from inflation?

Holding cash or low-interest savings accounts guarantees real loss of purchasing power during inflation. Productive assets like diversified equities (stocks), real estate, inflation-protected bonds (TIPS, I-Bonds), and commodities historically provide returns higher than inflation.
