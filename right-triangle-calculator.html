The Fundamentals of Right-Angled Triangles

A right triangle (or right-angled triangle) is a three-sided polygon where one interior angle measures exactly 90 degrees (π/2 radians). The side opposite the 90-degree angle is always the longest side, termed the hypotenuse. The two shorter perpendicular sides are known as the legs or catheti.

The Pythagorean Theorem

Named after the ancient Greek philosopher Pythagoras, the theorem establishes that the area of the square built upon the hypotenuse equals the sum of the areas of the squares on the remaining two legs:

a2 + b2 = c2
c = √(a2 + b2)
a = √(c2 − b2)

Trigonometric Ratios (SOH CAH TOA)

The fundamental trigonometric relationships for angle α are expressed as ratios of side lengths:

  • Sine (sin α): Opposite / Hypotenuse = a / c
  • Cosine (cos α): Adjacent / Hypotenuse = b / c
  • Tangent (tan α): Opposite / Adjacent = a / b
  • Altitude to Hypotenuse (hc): (a × b) / c

Special Right Triangles

Certain right triangles appear with high frequency in engineering and architecture:

  1. 30°-60°-90° Triangle: Side lengths are in the ratio 1 : √3 : 2 (approximately 1 : 1.732 : 2).
  2. 45°-45°-90° Triangle: An isosceles right triangle with side length ratio 1 : 1 : √2 (approximately 1 : 1 : 1.414).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Pythagorean theorem?

The Pythagorean theorem states that in any right-angled triangle, the square of the length of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the lengths of the two other legs: a^2 + b^2 = c^2.

How do you find the hypotenuse of a right triangle?

To find the hypotenuse (c), take the square root of the sum of the squares of both legs: c = sqrt(a^2 + b^2).

What is the 3-4-5 right triangle rule?

The 3-4-5 rule is the simplest Pythagorean triple where legs of length 3 and 4 produce a hypotenuse of exactly 5 (3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5^2). Builders and carpenters use this rule extensively to square framing and foundations.

What are the six basic trigonometric ratios?

The six standard trigonometric functions for acute angle alpha are: sine (opposite/hypotenuse), cosine (adjacent/hypotenuse), tangent (opposite/adjacent), cosecant (hypotenuse/opposite), secant (hypotenuse/adjacent), and cotangent (adjacent/opposite).
