Complete Guide to Letter Cases and Grammatical Conventions

Whether crafting an essay headline, drafting professional communications, or naming variables in a software repository, case formatting establishes consistency, readability, and brand professionalism. Manually re-typing content that was typed with the Caps Lock key engaged or converting database field names into web URLs is tedious and prone to typos.

Common Text Cases Explained

  • UPPERCASE (ALL CAPS): Every letter is converted to capital form. Frequently utilized for acronyms, legal disclosures, and prominent warnings.
  • lowercase: All letters are normalized to minuscule characters. Common for clean email addresses, URLs, and Unix commands.
  • Title Case: The standard format for book titles, movie names, and article headlines. The first letter of major words is capitalized, while non-initial articles and conjunctions (such as a, an, the, and, but, or, for, in, of) remain lowercase.
  • Sentence case: The standard written English style where only the very first letter of a sentence and recognized proper nouns are capitalized.
  • camelCase: The words are concatenated without spaces, and each word following the first begins with an uppercase letter (e.g. userProfileData).
  • snake_case: All words are lowercased and joined using underscores (e.g. user_profile_data).
  • kebab-case: Words are lowercased and separated with hyphens (e.g. user-profile-data). Favored in URL slugs and CSS class definitions.
