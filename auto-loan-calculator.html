How to Calculate Your Auto Loan Payment

An auto loan is an installment loan where you borrow money to purchase a motor vehicle and repay the balance over fixed monthly intervals with interest. The monthly payment is calculated using the standard fixed amortization formula:

Monthly Payment (M) = P × [r(1 + r)n] / [(1 + r)n − 1]

Where:

  • P (Principal) = Vehicle Price − Down Payment − Net Trade-in Equity + Sales Tax + Dealer Fees
  • r (Monthly Interest Rate) = Annual Percentage Rate (APR) / 100 / 12
  • n (Total Payments) = Loan Term in Months (e.g., 60 for a 5-year loan)

The 20/4/10 Rule for Smart Car Buying

Automobiles are rapidly depreciating assets, losing an average of 15% to 20% of their value in the very first year. To prevent becoming "underwater" (where you owe more to the bank than the car is worth on the market), financial planners recommend the 20/4/10 rule:

  1. 20% Down Payment: Putting down at least 20% in cash or trade-in equity absorbs the steep initial depreciation curve.
  2. 4-Year Maximum Term (48 Months): Financing for 48 months or fewer ensures you build equity faster than the vehicle depreciates.
  3. 10% Income Cap: Your total monthly vehicle expenses—including loan payment, insurance premiums, maintenance, and fuel—should never exceed 10% of your gross monthly income.

How Trade-In Affects Sales Tax

In over 40 US states and several international jurisdictions, a vehicle trade-in provides a significant tax credit. Sales tax is computed only on the difference between the new vehicle's purchase price and the trade-in allowance. For example, buying a $35,000 car with a $5,000 trade-in at a 6% tax rate results in $1,800 in sales tax instead of $2,100, saving you $300 immediately.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does a trade-in affect my car loan and sales tax?

In most US states and regions, your trade-in vehicle's value directly reduces the taxable purchase price of the new vehicle. For instance, trading in a $5,000 vehicle for a $30,000 car means you only pay sales tax on the $25,000 difference, saving you hundreds in upfront tax.

What is the recommended car loan term length?

Financial advisors recommend loan terms between 36 and 60 months. While 72 or 84-month loans lower your monthly payment, they significantly increase total interest paid and keep you underwater (owing more than the car is worth) for years.

What is the 20/4/10 rule for buying a car?

The 20/4/10 rule recommends putting down at least 20%, financing the vehicle for no longer than 4 years (48 months), and keeping total monthly automotive expenses (loan payment, insurance, fuel) below 10% of your gross monthly income.

What is GAP insurance and do I need it?

Guaranteed Asset Protection (GAP) insurance covers the difference between your vehicle's actual cash market value and the remaining loan balance if your car is stolen or totaled. It is highly recommended if you make a down payment under 20% or finance for 60+ months.
