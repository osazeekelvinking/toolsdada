Understanding Scientific & Engineering Notation

In chemistry, physics, and astronomy, numbers can be unwieldy: the mass of an electron is $0.00000000000000000000000000000091093837$ kg, while the speed of light is $299,792,458$ m/s. Scientific notation condenses these numbers into the form:

m × 10n (where 1 ≤ |m| < 10 and n is an integer)

Rules for Exponent Arithmetic

  • Multiplication: Multiply the coefficients and add the exponents:
    (a × 10x) × (b × 10y) = (a × b) × 10x + y
  • Division: Divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents:
    (a × 10x) ÷ (b × 10y) = (a / b) × 10x − y
  • Addition and Subtraction: Convert one or both numbers so they share the exact same power of 10, then add/subtract coefficients:
    (a × 10x) + (b × 10x) = (a + b) × 10x

Engineering Notation & SI Prefixes

Unlike scientific notation where the exponent can be any integer, engineering notation restricts the exponent to multiples of 3 ($10^3, 10^6, 10^9, 10^{-3}, 10^{-6}, \dots$). This allows immediate matching with International System of Units (SI) metric prefixes:

  • $10^9$: Giga (G)
  • $10^6$: Mega (M)
  • $10^3$: kilo (k)
  • $10^{-3}$: milli (m)
  • $10^{-6}$: micro (μ)
  • $10^{-9}$: nano (n)

Frequently Asked Questions

What is scientific notation?

Scientific notation is a method of writing very large or very small numbers compactly in the form a * 10^b, where the coefficient a (significand) is between 1 and 10 (1 <= |a| < 10) and b is an integer exponent.

How does engineering notation differ from scientific notation?

In engineering notation, the exponent is always constrained to an exact multiple of 3 (e.g. 10^3, 10^6, 10^-9), allowing direct alignment with International System of Units (SI) metric prefixes like kilo, Mega, micro, and nano.

How do you multiply two numbers in scientific notation?

Multiply the coefficients together and add the exponents: (a * 10^x) * (b * 10^y) = (a * b) * 10^(x + y). If the resulting coefficient is 10 or greater, normalize it by dividing by 10 and adding 1 to the exponent.

How do you add or subtract numbers in scientific notation?

To add or subtract, both numbers must first be rewritten so their exponents match. Once exponents are identical, add or subtract the coefficients and maintain the common power of 10, then normalize if needed.
