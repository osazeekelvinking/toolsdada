How Daily Calorie & TDEE Calculations Work

Your body burns calories through three primary mechanisms: your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), the Thermic Effect of Food (TEF), and Physical Activity Expenditure.

The Mifflin-St Jeor Formula

Our calculator computes your BMR using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, widely regarded by clinicians and sports scientists as the gold standard:

  • For Men: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) - 5 × age (years) + 5
  • For Women: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) - 5 × age (years) - 161

Your BMR is then multiplied by your activity coefficient (from 1.2 for sedentary up to 1.9 for extreme athletics) to determine your TDEE.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the base energy required just to stay alive at complete rest. TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) multiplies BMR by your physical activity factor to determine your total daily burn.

How many calories deficit is safe for fat loss?

A standard, sustainable deficit is 500 calories per day (equivalent to losing ~1 pound of body fat per week). Deficits larger than 1,000 kcal/day should only be undertaken under medical guidance.

Which equation is most accurate for calories?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is recognized by the American Dietetic Association as the most reliable standard for predicting resting metabolic rate in healthy individuals.
