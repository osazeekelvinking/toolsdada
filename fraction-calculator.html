How to Perform Fraction Operations

A fraction represents a part of a whole or any number of equal parts. It consists of a top integer (the numerator) and a non-zero bottom integer (the denominator).

1. Adding and Subtracting Fractions

To add or subtract fractions, they must share a common denominator. If denominators differ, find their Least Common Multiple (LCM), which serves as the Least Common Denominator (LCD):

a/b ± c/d = (a × d ± c × b) / (b × d)

2. Multiplying Fractions

Multiplication is straightforward: multiply numerators together and multiply denominators together:

(a / b) × (c / d) = (a × c) / (b × d)

3. Dividing Fractions (Keep, Change, Flip)

To divide one fraction by another, multiply the dividend by the reciprocal (inverse) of the divisor:

(a / b) ÷ (c / d) = (a / b) × (d / c) = (a × d) / (b × c)

Simplifying to Lowest Terms

A fraction is in its simplest form when the numerator and denominator are co-prime (their Greatest Common Divisor, or GCD, is 1). To simplify any fraction, compute the GCD using the Euclidean algorithm and divide both numerator and denominator by it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you add or subtract fractions with different denominators?

To add or subtract fractions with unlike denominators, first find the Least Common Denominator (LCD). Convert both fractions to equivalent fractions using this LCD, add or subtract the numerators while keeping the denominator unchanged, and simplify the final fraction to lowest terms.

How do you multiply fractions?

To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators together to form the new numerator, and multiply the denominators together to form the new denominator: (a/b) * (c/d) = (a*c)/(b*d). Then reduce by the greatest common divisor (GCD).

How do you divide fractions?

To divide by a fraction, invert the divisor fraction (find its reciprocal) and multiply: (a/b) / (c/d) = (a/b) * (d/c) = (a*d)/(b*c).

What is the difference between an improper fraction and a mixed number?

An improper fraction has a numerator greater than or equal to its denominator (e.g. 7/4). A mixed number combines a non-zero whole integer with a proper fraction (e.g. 1 3/4). Both represent the exact same mathematical value.
